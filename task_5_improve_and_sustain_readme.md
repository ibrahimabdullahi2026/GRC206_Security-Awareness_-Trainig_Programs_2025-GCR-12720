## Overview & Planning Assumptions

This document details **Task 5 (Improve and Sustain)** of a 12-month security awareness and training program for **Northbridge Logistics**, structured in accordance with the lifecycle approach in **NIST SP 800-50r1** (Merritt et al., 2024).

> **Note:** Facts taken directly from the assignment brief are marked **(S)**. All other figures, headcounts, and targets are planning assumptions **(A1–A6)** or illustrative proposals rather than live Northbridge results.

* **A1 — Workforce Segments (Total: 800 (S)):** Finance, procurement, and payroll (35); executives and assistants (25); office-based staff including IT (215); remote sales (100); warehouse supervisors (45); warehouse operatives (380).
* **A2 — Systems:** Corporate email supports a one-click report button, MFA, and simulations; operatives mostly use shared handhelds and have little or no desk time.
* **A3 — Team:** Security Manager plus two analysts (the brief says "small" **(S)**); HR/L&D partner at 0.2 FTE; about 20 volunteer champions at roughly 2 hours a month.
* **A4 — Budget:** About USD 30,000 for the year for tools, printing, translation, and recognition, excluding salaries (the brief says "modest" **(S)**).
* **A5 — Authority:** The sponsor can release 10 paid minutes per shift per month; HR and Legal approve simulations and the staff notice.
* **A6 — Languages and Law:** English plus at least one local language on site; no jurisdiction is stated, so applicable data-protection law is assumed to apply to the regulated customer data **(S)**.

## Task 5: Improve and Sustain

### 5.1 Quarterly Review Routine (Months 3, 6, 9 and 12)
A 60-minute meeting chaired by the Security Manager, attended by the sponsor, Finance Controller, Head of IT Operations, Warehouse Operations Manager, HR/L&D lead and two champions (one a warehouse supervisor). It examines the dashboard, lessons from incidents, simulation results, learner feedback and threat updates. Decisions and actions are recorded in a shared action log with owner, due date and status, and each review opens by checking the previous actions.

**Triggers for content updates:**
1. A confirmed incident or near miss with a human cause, with a micro-update within 14 days.
2. An emerging scam seen internally or in advisories, such as vendor impersonation, voice cloning or SMS lures.
3. A process or system change, such as a new payment portal or MFA change.
4. A metric off target for two reviews running, such as report rate below target or verification under 90%.
5. Repeated themes in learner feedback.

### 5.2 Listening to Learners
* **Feedback Collection:** A two-question feedback card or QR code follows every activity.
* **Listening Circles:** Champions run a 30-minute listening circle on each shift every quarter, including nights and weekends.
* **Anonymous Feedback:** Feedback can also arrive anonymously through the reporting route.
* **Surveys & Closing the Loop:** Surveys in Months 2, 6 and 12 allow comparison by audience, and each quarter a "you said, we did" notice shows what changed.

### 5.3 Recognising Good Behaviour Without Shaming
* **"Great Catch" Recognition:** Each month, with the person’s consent, a short note names the person or team that made a useful report, with a thank-you from the sponsor and a small non-cash token.
* **Near-Miss Stories:** In quarterly team meetings, volunteers who made a mistake and reported it quickly are thanked and their lesson is shared, anonymised unless they choose otherwise. Recognition is never used for ranking or league tables.

### 5.4 Keeping It Sustainable for a Small Team
Estimated effort is about 0.3 FTE for the Security Manager and 0.2 FTE for analysts (simulations and triage), 0.2 FTE for HR/L&D, 2 hours a month per champion and 10 minutes per shift per month for supervisors. No audience gets more than two activities in any month, and the calendar spreads simulations and talks so that preparation, delivery and follow-up never land in the same weeks.
