## Overview & Planning Assumptions

This document details **Task 4 (Measure)** of a 12-month security awareness and training program for **Northbridge Logistics**, structured in accordance with the lifecycle approach in **NIST SP 800-50r1** (Merritt et al., 2024).

> **Note:** Facts taken directly from the assignment brief are marked **(S)**. All other figures, headcounts, and targets are planning assumptions **(A1–A6)** or illustrative proposals rather than live Northbridge results.

* **A1 — Workforce Segments (Total: 800 (S)):** Finance, procurement, and payroll (35); executives and assistants (25); office-based staff including IT (215); remote sales (100); warehouse supervisors (45); warehouse operatives (380).
* **A2 — Systems:** Corporate email supports a one-click report button, MFA, and simulations; operatives mostly use shared handhelds and have little or no desk time.
* **A3 — Team:** Security Manager plus two analysts (the brief says "small" **(S)**); HR/L&D partner at 0.2 FTE; about 20 volunteer champions at roughly 2 hours a month.
* **A4 — Budget:** About USD 30,000 for the year for tools, printing, translation, and recognition, excluding salaries (the brief says "modest" **(S)**).
* **A5 — Authority:** The sponsor can release 10 paid minutes per shift per month; HR and Legal approve simulations and the staff notice.
* **A6 — Languages and Law:** English plus at least one local language on site; no jurisdiction is stated, so applicable data-protection law is assumed to apply to the regulated customer data **(S)**.

## Task 4: Measure

Metrics separate reach (who was covered), behaviour (what people do) and outcomes (what happens). Each is compared with the Month 1–2 baseline.

| Metric (type) | Calculation | Data source | Owner | Review |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **M1 Role-relevant participation (reach)** | Staff who completed their audience’s scheduled activity ÷ staff assigned, by audience and shift. | HR learning records; toolbox attendance sheets. | HR/L&D lead | Monthly |
| **M2 Report rate and time-to-report (behaviour)** | Simulated messages reported ÷ delivered; median minutes from delivery to report; count of real reports. | Simulation platform; report-button and hotline logs. | Security Manager | Each simulation; real reports monthly |
| **M3 Payment verification rate (behaviour)** | Bank-detail changes and urgent payment requests verified by call-back ÷ total in the month. | Accounts payable change log; audit. | Finance Controller | Monthly |
| **M4 Credential hygiene (behaviour)** | Share of accounts with MFA; share using the approved password manager; breached or reused passwords found at screening. | Identity-provider report; password-manager console; screening. | Head of IT Operations | Monthly |
| **M5 Human-error incidents (outcome)** | Confirmed incidents with a human-behaviour cause (phishing or reuse compromise, mis-sent data, payment fraud) per 100 staff, with median time to report and contain. | Incident register. | Security Manager | Quarterly |
| **M6 Fraud outcome (outcome)** | Invoice-fraud attempts that reached payment stage; value paid versus value stopped. | Finance records; incident register. | Finance Controller | Quarterly |
| **M7 Reporting climate (behaviour)** | Share agreeing “I know how to report” and “I would report a mistake without fear”. | Pulse survey (paper or QR for warehouse). | HR/L&D lead | Months 2, 6, 12 |

### What a Change Would Mean
* **M1 high but M2 and M3 flat:** Content is reaching people but not changing behaviour, so revisit the format or the supporting control. Low M1 on one shift points to a scheduling or access barrier, not unwillingness.
* **M2 and real reports rising:** More incidents will be seen, so M5 may rise at first. It is read with severity and time-to-contain, and is treated as a sign of a healthier reporting culture rather than failure.
* **Clicks falling without more reporting:** Treated with caution; click rate is recorded but is not a target (Ho et al., 2025).
* **M3 and M6 together** show whether the call-back control and awareness are protecting money; M4 separates technical adoption from awareness.

### Reporting to Leadership
* **Monthly:** A one-page dashboard (trend against baseline, red-amber-green) to the sponsor and department heads. 
* **Quarterly:** A 30-minute briefing inside the review, stating limitations (small numbers within some audiences, simulations are not real attacks, survey answers are self-reported, the baseline is only two months) and the decisions needed, such as protecting shift time, enforcing the call-back rule or funding. 
* **Year-end:** A year-end report compares Month 12 with the baseline and recommends whether to continue.
